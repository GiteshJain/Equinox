# Build spec: the scripts and the website onto the library and the figures (#18 build, pull request 4) — 13 Oct 2026

**Why:** step 4 of the build order (`docs/design/2026-10-12-planner-app.md` §13), decision #19. The library (pull
request 2) and the captured figures (pull request 3) are live; now the other places that work out the same things
stop doing it themselves:
- the optimiser (`scripts/planning/plan.py`) is replaced by the library;
- the weekly report reads the database's figures, so the retired surplus rule and group totals go (manual §3, §1);
- the website's trip search uses the library's connection rule, with the 8-hour wait (#19).

The game is paused and the old planner frozen (manual §1): **nothing in `web/planner/` changes.**

**Outcome when done:**
- `scripts/planning/plan.py` is gone; Claude runs `node lib/rules/dist/cli.js optimise …` instead.
- The weekly report's numbers come from `export_for_report('figures')` and `('rules')`. It has no surplus figure, no
  group totals, no airline names, game IDs or hubs typed into the scripts, and no week-specific text in the code.
- The website's trip search finds connections with the library's rule: at least the airport's minimum connection time,
  at most `connection_max_wait_h` (8 hours, was 10).
- Every check passes, and the figures the report prints equal the database's.

## What to build
The design is the specification: §7 (the library's callers) and §8 (the figures' callers). Where the design, the manual
and this spec disagree, stop and say so. The manual wins on every rule.

### 1. The optimiser
- Delete `scripts/planning/plan.py`. `scripts/README.md`'s "schedule optimiser" section says how Claude runs the
  library's `optimise` command instead, with one worked example, and where its input comes from
  (`export_for_report('rules')`).
- `tests/rules_planpy_test.py` keeps its case: the library's best plan must equal **plan.py's saved answer** (KCH, PQC,
  PEN from 06:45, 70 connections, maintenance 289%), stored with the fixture
  (`tests/fixtures/optimiser_kul_2026-10-10.json`). Rename the test to say what it now proves.

### 2. The weekly report (`scripts/report/`)
- **`pull.py`** also fetches `export_for_report('figures')` and `('rules')` into `figures.json` and `rules.json`.
- **`analyze.py`:**
  - our airlines, their game IDs, names and bases come from `rules.json` (`airlines`, `airline_bases`), not the
    `OURS`, `PL_NAME`, `HUBS` and `AL` lists in the code. Nothing assumes six (#14);
  - the per-airline figures (aircraft, routes, flights and seats a day, block hours a day) come from the stored legs
    through the library's `aircraftWeek`, not the 846 km/h formula;
  - the money (cash, profit a day, flight profit, the lowest point in 14 days, next order, route profit, growth
    signals, hub progress) comes from `figures.json`. **No surplus, no fixed-costs-minus-cash figure, no group
    totals** (manual §1, §3);
  - competition and markets keep using the world scan as now.
- **`build.py`:**
  - every figure from `results.json`; no figure typed into the code;
  - the PDF's name and week come from the date (ISO week), not `W41` in the code;
  - the week's written text (headlines, risks, the plan, rivals' reads, the timeline) comes from a `notes.json` the
    Saturday review writes. The script lays out what it's given and leaves out a section with no notes.
    `scripts/report/notes.example.json` shows the format;
  - the finance table per airline: cash, profit a day, flight profit a day, lowest point in 14 days and when, next
    order. No group row.
- **`charts.py`:** names and colours from `results.json`.
- **A test** (`tests/report_test.py`, in `run_all.sh`): `analyze.py`, `charts.py` and `build.py` run end to end on a
  small dated sample in `tests/fixtures/` (the figures and rules exported from the rehearsal's copy, cut to what the
  report needs, plus a small scan sample), with `notes.example.json`. Checks:
  - the PDF is made;
  - its text has no "surplus";
  - each airline's cash, profit a day, lowest point and next order in the PDF equal the sample's figures;
  - the airlines listed are exactly the sample's.
- `scripts/README.md` updated: the new files, `notes.json`, and that the report's money is the database's.
- **Not in this pull request:** gander monthly (`scripts/newsletter/`) and the scanner. Note in the pull request
  anything in them that assumes six airlines or types in an issue name.

### 3. The website's trip search (`web/assets/`)
- The build also writes the library as one browser file, `web/assets/rules.js` (gitignored, built by Cloudflare like
  `web/app/`), exposing what the trip search needs.
- `site_public()` gains `content.connection_max_wait_h` (from `settings`), in a small migration. Its size grows by a few
  bytes (#20's trigger is 500 KB).
- `findTrips` in `site.js` uses the library's connection test for every transfer: from the airport's `mct` (90 minutes
  where unknown, as now) to `connection_max_wait_h`. Nothing else on the website changes.
- `tests/site_test.py`: a trip with a 9-hour transfer, found before, is no longer offered; one at exactly the minimum
  connection time is; the page loads `rules.js` with no errors, and works on the feed without the new key (falls back
  to 8 hours).

## Rules for the helper
- Follow `CLAUDE.md`. Read `.claude/skills/supabase-postgres-best-practices/SKILL.md` for the migration.
- **Never connect to the live database or use the Supabase connector tools.** Test by pushing and reading the *checks*
  and *Database rehearsal* results. Docker is available here.
- **Don't change `web/planner/`.** In `web/assets/`, change only `site.js`'s trip search and what loads `rules.js`.
- Game data only as small dated samples in `tests/fixtures/`.
- Work on branch `callers`, open the pull request with the REST API (GraphQL is blocked), and don't merge.
