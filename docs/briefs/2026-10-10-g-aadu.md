## Background
- **Loads and profit** (game_flights, 3–10 Oct): every Virgin route sold out except Cairo (97% economy), Oslo (96%) and
  Toulouse (98%). Best profit a flight: Cairo 40k, Istanbul 25k, **Manchester–Rome 22k and Manchester–Madrid 22k (both
  once daily)**, Malaga 22k, Lisbon 20k, Venice 18k, Prague 17k, Marseille 16k, Lyon 15k. Edinburgh's E195 routes earn
  8–12k.
- **Money** (capture 11 Oct 04:35 UTC): 7.50M cash; flight profit +1.22M a day before leases (15k a flight × 82).
  G-AADU's lease starts on 16 Oct whichever option is chosen.
- **Bases** (flight_legs): Gatwick 224 departures a week, 301 once the four waiting cards fly (of 750 under hubs first);
  Manchester 56; Edinburgh 35. Hubs first governs opening new hubs only; Manchester is open and contested.
- **Competition** (world scan, 6 Oct): no rival on any route in A–E. At Gatwick easyJet has 385 departures a week to our
  224. At Manchester we're largest (56), ahead of Transavia 42, British Caledonian 35 and British Airways 21. At
  Edinburgh KLM Cityhopper has 42 and TAP flies Lisbon twice daily.
- **Note:** on 7 Oct Claude suggested keeping Manchester at two aircraft. It was never adopted as a rule and isn't in the
  manual; D or E would make Manchester three aircraft.

## Scenarios and the risk of waiting
- **D goes well:** Istanbul fills like Gatwick's (three dailies, all sold out), and Manchester gains a long premium route
  no one else flies. **Badly:** Istanbul runs at 70–80% and earns about 10k a flight; Rome alone still pays.
- **E** is the safe money (two proven routes) but adds only a 4-star market. **A** earns similarly but flies only 9 hours.
- **Waiting:** the aircraft is parked; each day costs roughly 90k of flight profit, and its lease starts on 16 Oct.

## What would change the recommendation
- Holding Manchester at two aircraft: then B (Gatwick, proven routes plus one new market).
- Preferring profit over growth this time: E.
- A rival appearing on Manchester–Istanbul in the next scan: E.
