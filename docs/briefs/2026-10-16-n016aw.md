"frequency comes first: second dailies on the sold-out Atlantic and transcon routes"). An A321neo can't reach Europe

## Background
- **Pan Am** (briefing, 16 Oct 02:57 UTC): profit 0.95M a day over 7 days, load 99%, cash 5.76M with 5.03M at the
  lowest point in 14 days. 13 routes are on the "ready for another flight" list; Toronto is the only one to fix.
- **Hubs first:** JFK has 154 departures a week of 750, the furthest of any main hub. All three new aircraft are JFK's.
- **Rotation:** the last two moves were big markets (Chicago O'Hare, 30 Sep; Gatwick, 5 Oct), so frequency is next.
  Growth matters as much as profit (manual §3), so the batch also opens new cities: Miami here.
- **Open five-star trunks from JFK** (routes list; 6 Oct world scan, 10 days old): Minneapolis, Miami, Dallas and
  Las Vegas with no rival; Houston (Eastern 3 a week) and Denver (Eastern 2); Phoenix (US Airways 42, contested).
- **San Francisco timing:** the new daily leaves JFK at 06:14, 31 minutes before the existing AW1007 (06:45). Allowed
  since 15 Oct (at least Gander's 5 minutes, placed for connections, manual §4); the game's demand ignores time of day.
- **Ruled out:** San Francisco with Dallas or Cancún leave no room for maintenance (the optimiser found no feasible day).

## Scenarios and the risk of waiting
- **A goes well:** the second SFO daily sells like the first and Miami like Orlando: about 128k a day.
  **Badly:** the second SFO daily drops to 80% and Miami sells like Detroit (13.7k): still about 100k a day, three
  times the lease.
- **Waiting** leaves a delivered aircraft idle at about 0.1M a day.
