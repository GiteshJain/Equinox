## Background
- **Hubs first** (manual §3): until Delhi reaches 750 weekly departures (279 today, 273 once Delhi–JFK stops), other
  bases get aircraft only for contested routes. Mumbai is contested only on its Gulf routes (Etihad 67 a week on
  Abu Dhabi, Qatar 56 on Doha, Emirates 28 on Dubai, Saudia 44 on Jeddah), which VT-ABF (BOM) and VT-ABH (BOM) already
  take; nothing else at Mumbai is allowed today.
- **Why Mumbai** (manual §3, market share first): after Delhi it has the most openings (159, 41 of them 5-star) and the
  most rival flying on its pairs (224 departures a week). Bengaluru has 127 (27 five-star), Hyderabad 131 (22), Chennai
  and Kolkata 102 each (18).
- **Mumbai's first routes:** Hyderabad (623 km), Bengaluru (834), Chennai (1,033), Kolkata (1,666), all 5-star except
  Kolkata (4-star), no rival on any; then Riyadh, Bangkok and Hong Kong (5-star). Each aircraft's routes come in the
  evening proposal, by the default line and the optimiser.
- **Delhi meanwhile:** every Delhi route sells out (route profit, 7 days), so A remains a good use of the money;
  Delhi keeps receiving Kingfisher's own earnings from 1 Nov (its trunks to Hyderabad, Chennai, Mumbai, Bengaluru and
  Kolkata).
- **Prices with the cabin:** A320neo SHARP 2.22M, E195-E2 1.87M (the cash forecast, 26 Oct).

## Scenarios and the risk of waiting
- **Waiting to 1 Nov (C)** saves the rule exception but leaves both lines idle for about four days and limits Mumbai
  to four domestic trunks.
- **If Mumbai's first routes sell poorly** (VT-ABF's Gulf routes are the first read, from about 2 Nov), the later
  aircraft can still be pointed at Delhi before they're ordered.

## What would change the recommendation
- Gitesh prefers keeping hubs first intact: C.
- Delhi's banks need the departures first (connections over local markets): A.
- A rival starts building at Mumbai: B, sooner.
