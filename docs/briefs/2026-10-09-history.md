# History — 9 Oct 2026 (decision #15)

**Decision:** what the new tables (#13) remember about the past. That means both how your plans and settings changed,
and how each airline's numbers moved: fleet, departures, cash, loads and market share.
**Recommendation:** C, which has two parts.
- **A change log:** every change to a plan or setting is recorded with its old and new values, when it happened and
  who made it (you in the planner, Claude, the capture or a script).
- **Daily snapshots:** one row a day per airline with the numbers that matter, kept for good, plus market share from
  each world scan.

It means any change can be undone in minutes, and the weekly report and gander monthly get real trends. It's part of
the #13 build, about one extra pull request.

## Background
**What history exists today** (live database and repository, 9 Oct):
- **Nightly backups:** each night's whole database goes to `equinox-backups` at 07:17 UTC, and GitHub keeps every
  night. It's a restore point, not something to query: finding what changed in one plan card means restoring a copy.
- **Plans and settings:** none in the database. The planner's one row (`planner_state`) is overwritten on every save.
  An earlier idea, copying that row on each save, was set aside for the foundation plan: 194 KB a copy.
- **Dated data the capture keeps:**
  - cash and accounts from the balance sheet, since 7 Oct (3 days);
  - the income statement, since 7 Oct;
  - flights flown with their loads, since 6 Oct (935 flights);
  - the planner also kept daily cash by hand from 29 Sep.
- **Fleet and departures over time:** nothing. The planner only knows today's fleet and schedule.
- **Market share:** only one world scan is kept (6 Oct). Scans 1 and 2 were deleted earlier to save space, so
  there's no trend yet. Each scan stores every airline's full schedule (about 800 KB), which is why old ones were
  dropped.
- **Scheduling:** the database can run its own nightly jobs. `pg_cron` is available on the free plan, not yet
  switched on.

**Why this is cheap with tables and wasn't before:** with one 194 KB row, keeping history meant a full copy per click.
With #13's tables, a change touches a few rows of a few hundred bytes each. One condition: the adapter that keeps the
old planner working must save only the rows that actually changed. If it rewrote every row on each save, every click
would log hundreds of rows.

## What C would hold
- **The change log:** one table. For each change to a planner table (aircraft, flights, routes, plans, to-dos,
  settings, world rules) it records the table, the item, old and new values, the time, and who made it:
  - the planner (your sign-in);
  - Claude (direct database edits);
  - the capture;
  - a script.

  The capture's tables aren't logged; they're dated already.
- **Daily snapshots,** taken each night by a database job:
  - **per airline:** aircraft by type, weekly departures and seats, destinations, cash, fixed costs for the next 7
    days, average load and the day's profit;
  - **per scan:** each airline's weekly departures and destinations in the world, so market share by airport and by
    market can be tracked. The summary is kept even when a scan's full schedules are later cleared (#16).

  Everything is keyed by world and airline (#14).
- **Backfill at the start, from what's kept:**
  - cash from 29 Sep;
  - flights flown and loads from 6 Oct;
  - the 6 Oct scan;
  - the fleet from delivery dates, where they're known (from 28 Sep).
- **What it gives:**
  - **Undo:** Claude can put any item back as it was at a given time. Later, the new planner (#18) can show an item's
    history.
  - **Questions answered:** who changed this plan card and when; what the schedule looked like last Tuesday.
  - **Trends** for the weekly report and gander monthly: fleet growth, departures, cash and market share week on week.

## Scenarios and the risk of waiting
- **A bad save or a wrong edit:**
  - under C, find it in the log and undo it in minutes;
  - under A or B, restore last night's backup into a copy and copy the item back. That takes longer, and anything
    since last night is gone.
- **The weekly report asks how Pan Am's share at JFK moved:**
  - under B or C it's a snapshot query;
  - under A, there's nothing to compare with.
- **Waiting:** history can't be made up later. Every week without snapshots is a week missing from every trend, and
  the change log only covers changes after it starts. The #13 build is the natural moment, since the tables are new
  anyway.

## What would change the recommendation
- **If you never expect to undo a single change** and only want trends: B, slightly less to build.
- **If you want to see the whole planner as it was on a past date, often:** D. C can rebuild a past state from the log
  when it's needed, so this seems unlikely to be worth it.
- **#16** decides how long the detail is kept, including the change log. Snapshots are small enough to keep for good.
- **#22** decides when the world scan runs. Market share is only as frequent as the scans.
