# The fare ladder under launch: judged by loads — 26 Oct 2026

**Decision:** how a cabin that jumped under launch is judged afterwards.
**Decided 26 Oct 2026 (Gitesh, Building chat): option A, his own proposal:** by loads, like any direction. "It's not
about more money, it's about keeping flights full while making the most we can."

| | **A. By loads (chosen)** | B. By revenue a flight (the rule since 23 Oct) |
|---|---|---|
| A cabin full at 60% that earned less than at launch | Climbs a step at a time (60 → 65 → 70…, a step every two weeks with the hold after a step up), full all the way | Reverts to launch at once, often half empty |
| Revenue | Below launch's for a few weeks, rising each step | Launch's at once |
| Loads | Kept full | Can fall back to where they started |
| Market share first (§3) | Fits: full flights, the most each step allows | Gives up full flights for money |
| Build | Medium: the ladder's under-launch part simplified | None |
| Easy to undo | Yes | — |

## The rule (manual §5)
After its first jump (80%, Ryanair 75%) and its 2-week hold, the cabin is judged on its loads like any direction: sold
out, it climbs a step (holding the next Saturday) on through launch to the cap; 90–99% sold out, it holds; under 90% sold
out, it jumps to 60% and holds 2 weeks. At 60% and still under 90% sold out it holds and is marked cut or keep? for
Gitesh; a route that isn't losing money is most likely kept. It never jumps again. No revenue test, no revert.

## Background
- Raised while deciding whether a revert should start the hold after a step up (the same evening's audit): a cabin
  reverted to launch would be judged the next Saturday on a week booked mostly at 60%.
- Under B, a revert happened only when 60% earned less a flight than launch (e.g. launch 75% full: 100 × 0.75 = 75
  against 60 × 1.00 = 60); the 80% jump had already earned less too. Under A such a cabin climbs back step by step.
- With no reverts, the hold-after-a-revert question falls away.

## What would change it
- Cabins climbing back from 60% for many weeks at visibly lower revenue than launch: consider a bigger step under launch.
