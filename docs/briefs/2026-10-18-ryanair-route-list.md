## Background
- **Ryanair** (briefing, 18 Oct 22:16 UTC): 25 aircraft, 168 flights a day, profit 1.09M a day over 7 days, load 100%;
  all 57 routes sold out over the last 7 days. Cash 1.03M, the lowest point now; the next order is safe from 19 Oct, and
  its profit pays for an aircraft about every 1.9 days, so about seven aircraft in two weeks. Nine are listed.
- **Where profit comes from:** routes of 1,000–2,000 km earn most a flight (Lisbon 23–26k, Porto 13–23k, Valencia 19k,
  Athens 17k); short hops least (Amsterdam–Munich 3k, Amsterdam–Prague 4k). A shorter sector gives an aircraft four
  rotations a day instead of three.
- **Rivals at the bases** (hub progress): Paris, Air France 679 departures a week; Amsterdam, KLM 518; Barcelona, Air
  Europa 203.
- **Type:** the A321neo for every line: on routes that sell out it gives the most profit per dollar of aircraft (Ryanair's
  A321neos about 70k a day after lease against its E195s' 40–50k), and no all-economy A320neo layout is needed yet.
- **Left out:** UK cities (UK–Europe is Virgin's, manual §2); Milan and Munich as bases (not open); routes already on a
  card (Bilbao, Thessaloniki, Malta, Menorca, Tallinn, Corfu, Seville, Hamburg and Reykjavík from Paris; Porto, Stockholm
  and Madrid from Amsterdam).

## What would change it
- A rival moving onto a pair before its aircraft arrives, or a route that no longer fits the aircraft: that card goes to
  Gitesh as options (manual §4).
- If Barcelona–Vienna (75% last week) stays well under full, Barcelona's lines 5 and 8 move behind Paris and Amsterdam.
- New contested routes that fall short once their first 7 days are up get the usual fix or losing marks.

**18 Oct: B, agreed.** Gitesh: market share first, profit second, for the whole group; capturing the demand and holding
more slots leaves new competition no room to move in. Manual §3 updated the same day.
