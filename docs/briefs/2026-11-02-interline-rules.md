# Interline policy: board paper

*Scheduling chat, 2 Nov 2026. Raised by Gitesh: "lets rehaul how we interline, very strict right now". Rewritten the
same evening as an executive decision covering now and the build-out of our route lists (Gitesh: "make it a real
executive decision"; staff cost left out at his request).*

## The decision
How equinox. uses outside airlines to extend its network: whom our six airlines interline with, now and as the network
grows to the full route lists (manual §2, destination shape).

## Recommendation: B+, "feed first, never feed a top competitor"
1. **Our six airlines are the network.** They interline with each other, as today.
2. **Outside partners extend it where we can't or won't fly.** At every foreign airport an airline of ours serves
   outside our base countries, it signs the one local airline that adds the most onward places (3 or more) from that
   airport. This applies in any country, Europe and the Americas included.
3. **Never feed a direct competitor.** No partner that is one of that airline's three biggest competitors (the weekly
   report's, `competition()`), and none of the 11 hub carriers. In our own base countries the partner is our own
   airline.
4. **The partner follows the network.**
   - Each new foreign airport gets its partner proposed with the plan card that opens it.
   - Every Saturday the review re-checks partners against the latest scan and the competitor list.
   - A partner that becomes a top-3 competitor or a hub carrier is cancelled; the contract ends that accounting week.
5. **Ryanair included** (Gitesh to confirm): Gander rewards connections, and Ryanair gains the most of any airline.

## Why B+
- **Interlining only ever adds.** The game rates a connection below a direct flight. A partner gives passengers
  options we can't offer and doesn't pull them off our own direct flights. Today's rules leave ~85 of our ~115 foreign
  airports with local passengers only, and most of our routes already sell out, so the missing feed costs us fare level.
- **But it is mutual.** Every contract also feeds the partner. C and D would hand our passengers to the airlines we
  compete with hardest. B+ draws the line at the competitors the weekly report already tracks.
- **It stays right as we grow.** The rule picks partners by what they add beyond our own network. As our airlines
  open more of their lists, partners in markets we'll fly ourselves add less and drop off. Partners in markets we can
  never fly stay: Mexico, Canada, Japan, Indonesia, Australia, Russia and the Philippines, where cabotage blocks us.
- **It fits the group's shape.** Each airline owns its region and flow (§2). Partners extend those flows past our
  reach (Pan Am into Mexico and Canada, Malaysia into Indonesia and Australia, Virgin into the Nordics and Iberia)
  without crossing into another of our airlines' home markets.

## How it runs
- **Opening a foreign airport:** the plan card's note names the partner B+ picks. You send the request when you set
  up the aircraft, and Claude records it on the Interline screen.
- **Incoming requests:** accept if the airline is the B+ pick at one of our airports, or adds 3+ places there and
  isn't a top-3 competitor, hub carrier or based in one of our countries. Otherwise decline.
- **Saturday review:** partners that became top-3 competitors or hub carriers are listed for cancellation; airports
  without a partner are listed with the best candidate.
- **First Saturday of the month:** the partner book is reviewed with the strategy (bases, competitors).

## How we'll know it works
- **Fare level and loads** on routes to partnered airports against the rest, in the weekly report: partnered routes
  should climb the fare ladder faster.
- **Routes to partnered airports that don't sell out:** these should fill first.
- **Review at the 5 Dec monthly session:** four weeks of captures. If partnered routes show no lift, narrow to
  partners adding 10+ places. If partners fill our seats at lower fares than locals pay, partner only where we don't
  sell out.

## Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| A partner grows into a competitor | Weekly check against the top-3 list; cancel, effective that week |
| A partner player declines or never answers | It's a request, nothing lost; the next-best airline at that airport is asked instead |
| The game limits interlining for low-cost airlines (Ryanair) | Check on the first Ryanair request; if refused, Ryanair stays point-to-point |
| A big partner's connections crowd out higher-paying local passengers | The 5 Dec review measures it; narrow the rule if so |

## What would change the recommendation
- Evidence that partner passengers displace higher fares: partner only where routes don't sell out.
- A competitor group forming an alliance against us: reconsider partners belonging to it.
- The world's owner or the game limiting contracts: rank by onward places and keep the top.

## Background
**How interlining works** (AirlineSim handbook, "Interlining"):
- A contract links both airlines' whole networks at once, under our own connection rules.
- Connections rate below direct flights.
- Either side can cancel at any time; the contract ends with that accounting week.
- No limit on the number of partners is stated.
