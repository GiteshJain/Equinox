# G-AAEN, Virgin's new A321neo: its routes — 18 Oct 2026

**Decision:** what G-AAEN flies from Gatwick. It lands 19 Oct 03:34 UTC. It was ordered for frequency on sold-out routes
(17 Oct Virgin order brief), but until 31 Oct every new flight must open a new route (manual §4), and hubs first keeps it
at Gatwick (364 of 750 departures a week).
**Recommendation:** A, Budapest + Palma + Warsaw: three new 4-star cities, 99 links, 12.6 hours and healthy maintenance
(124%), for ~0.50M a week after the lease.

Four cities don't fit (maintenance), and Tel Aviv alone flies only 9.3 hours.

Profit is estimated from Virgin's own A321neo routes (route profit, 7 days to 18 Oct, the airline briefing), all sold
out: Lisbon, Malaga, Porto and Valencia (1,350–1,650 km) 20–21k a flight; Rome, Naples and Venice 16–17k; Frankfurt, Munich
and Zurich (650–950 km) 14–15k; Istanbul (2,500 km) 26.7k; Cairo 28.6k at 69% load. So Budapest, Warsaw and Palma about
17.5k, Helsinki and Reykjavik about 20k, Stuttgart about 14k, Tel Aviv about 32k. The A321neo's lease is about 0.24M a
week (manual §9).

## Background
- **Virgin** (briefing, 18 Oct 19:53 UTC): profit 0.72M a day over 7 days, load 98%, cash 3.08M (its lowest point is
  now). Every route sells out but Cairo (fix, 69%).
- **Competition** (world scan 4, 17 Oct): from Gatwick, easyJet flies Budapest twice a day and Warsaw, Palma, Berlin,
  Hamburg and Geneva once; Vienna 3 a day; Finnair Helsinki and Icelandair Reykjavik twice. Nobody flies Stuttgart or
  Tel Aviv.
- These are the 4-star cities still open from Gatwick in an A321neo's reach (manual §3, size by city).

## Scenarios and the risk of waiting
If all three sell out like Virgin's other 1,300–1,600 km routes, A earns about as much as Virgin's middle A321neo. Waiting
leaves the aircraft idle from 03:34 UTC.

## What would change the recommendation
- Wanting the most profit and accepting 102% maintenance: B.
- Wanting a first Middle East route now: D.
