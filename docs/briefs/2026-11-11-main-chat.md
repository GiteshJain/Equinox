## How B works

### Who does what
- **Main (Fable)** is the only chat Gitesh talks to. It holds the decisions and the briefs it puts to him, the To-do
  board, rule changes, the review of a worker's result, and "merge".
- **A worker (Opus)** does one job from a written brief and stops. There are three kinds, matching today's chats: a
  build, a scheduling job, a review job. A worker never talks to Gitesh and never decides for him.
- **A scheduled task** does routine work that needs nobody, like the newsroom roundups (on trial, to-do 227).

### A job, step by step
1. Gitesh asks for something, or a to-do comes due.
2. If it is small (under about five minutes, no long reading), Main does it itself: a to-do, a rule sentence, a quick
   figure from the database.
3. Otherwise Main writes the brief below and starts a worker in the background. Main tells Gitesh what is running and
   roughly how long it will take.
4. The worker reads what the job needs, does the work and stops. Its result is saved somewhere that lasts: a pull
   request, a plan card, a file in the repository, a to-do.
5. Its report comes back to Main, not to Gitesh: what it did, what it found (each figure with its source), what it
   could not do, and what needs a decision.
6. Main checks the figures a decision hangs on against the database, then tells Gitesh in a few lines.
7. When a job reaches a choice, the worker stops and sends back the options. Main puts them to Gitesh as a brief.
   With his answer, Main sends the same worker on, or starts a new one.
8. A build ends in a pull request. Main waits for the checks with one background wait, reads the pull request against
   its spec, and asks Gitesh for "merge". On "merge", Main merges, applies any database change to live as manual §1
   says, and checks it.

### Three examples
- **The speed fix (to-do 223).** Main starts a build worker. It comes back with the decision brief; Gitesh decides;
  the worker writes the spec and builds; the pull request opens; Main reviews it and asks for "merge".
- **An evening's orders.** Main starts a scheduling worker and keeps it for the sitting. It runs the cash forecast,
  the optimiser and the card check, and sends back the options. Main shows them as a table; Gitesh picks; the worker
  writes the plan cards; Gitesh sets the aircraft up in the game; Main checks them after the next capture.
- **Saturday.** The fare plan comes first (manual §7): Gitesh presses it on, and Main brings him the cut-or-keep
  decisions. A review worker writes the week's report from scratch; Main reads it and shows Gitesh the draft; he says
  publish.

### The brief Main gives a worker
```
Job: one sentence.
Why: what Gitesh wants, in his words where possible.
Read first: the files this job needs, and no others.
Facts: what Main already knows (figures with their source, ids, to-do numbers).
Do: the steps, or the result wanted.
Do not: decide anything that is Gitesh's; touch anything outside this job; push to main or merge;
        change the live database's structure; publish to the website unless this brief says so.
Stop and report when: you reach a choice; something outside the job looks wrong; the job is growing well past its size.
Report: what you did, what you found (figures with sources), what you could not do, what needs deciding,
        and any lesson the next worker of your kind should know. Short.
```

### What each reads
- **Main, when it starts:** the manual and `claude/handover-main.md`. Nothing else until a question needs it.
- **A build worker:** `CLAUDE.md` (which names the map, its part files and the manual) and its spec. It clones its own
  copy of the repository, as helpers do.
- **A scheduling worker:** the manual, the playbook's growth loop (§6) and `claude/handover-scheduling.md` for its
  tools and lessons.
- **A review worker:** the manual, the playbook's Saturday sections (§7 and §8) and `claude/handover-review.md`.

### During the trial
- The Building, Scheduling and Review chats stay as they are. Gitesh opens them only if he decides to go back.
- One build at a time, as now.
- The To-do board stays the one list. Titles keep "Building chat:", "Scheduling chat:" and "Review chat:", read as
  the kind of worker.
- A worker has no memory. A lesson it reports goes into that kind's handover note, which Main keeps up to date.
- Nothing else in the rulebook changes until the trial is judged.

## Background
- **Carrying between chats:** on 11 Nov the board held 8 open to-dos, and the three chats' notes held about 15 more
  open items with none (rule history, the To-do board as the one list). The double save above is one of them.
- **Reading before starting:** a Building chat read about 80,000 tokens before its first reply on 10 Nov, by the
  project's own count. Two changes on 11 Nov cut that: only the Building chat reads the decision log, and the map was
  slimmed (pull request 148).
- **Helpers:** since 8 Oct each sizeable build goes to a helper with a fresh memory and a written spec (manual §1).
  Pull requests 144 to 149 were built that way on 10 and 11 Nov.
- **The Audit chat did Main's job for two days,** on Fable: it decided with Gitesh, wrote rules and briefs, and passed
  work to the Building chat through the board. It also did its own reading, and filled once.
- **Fable before:** "Fable for business decisions" was the rule until 8 Oct, when "Opus for everything" replaced it
  (rule history, retired rules). Gitesh, 11 Nov: "We stopped using fable before because of usage because we were
  building everything fresh. I know we are still rebuilding again but i have s few resets now."
- **Usage: one measurement so far.** Starting a worker is the costly step, because it starts cold. A test worker on
  11 Nov, set to Opus, answered "Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5)" and used 69,457 tokens to do nothing but say so. So a
  job has to be big enough to be worth that start. Main cannot see Gitesh's plan usage; it can chart where its own
  session's went.
- **The model of a scheduled task cannot be set when it is made.** It takes the model of the chat that made it: the
  newsroom task runs on Fable.

## Scenarios and the risk of waiting
- **It goes well:** a week's work in one conversation, nothing carried by hand. On 18 Nov it becomes the rule: §1 is
  rewritten, the project instructions are pasted once more, and the handover notes become one note and three work
  sheets.
- **Usage is too high:** fewer workers (Main does more itself), or workers for builds and the report only, or back
  to A.
- **Main fills within the week even though it delegates:** a handover and a new Main chat. If that repeats, the split
  is not working.
- **A job is lost with Main's session:** its brief is in Main's conversation and its to-do is on the board. Main
  starts it again.
- **Waiting:** nothing breaks. The cost is the carrying and the re-reading, as today.

## What would change the recommendation
- The week's usage is more than Gitesh wants to spend: A, or B with workers for builds only.
- A worker's report can't be relied on unless Main does the work again: A.
- Scheduling sittings feel slower through a go-between: keep a Scheduling chat for sittings, and B for the rest.

## How the trial is judged, on 18 Nov
1. Did Gitesh have to open another chat?
2. Did anything get lost between jobs?
3. The week's usage as his plan shows it, and where Main's own went.
4. Did Main stay light enough to last the week?
